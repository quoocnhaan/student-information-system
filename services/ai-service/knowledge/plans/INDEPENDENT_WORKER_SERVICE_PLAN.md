# Central job service and shared worker plan

## Goal

Store background jobs in a **dedicated job service and its own database**, never in the database of Knowledge or another job-producing service. Build `services/job-service/` to own job rows, state transitions, publishing, replay, and status streams. Build `services/worker-service/` to consume and execute jobs for multiple services, starting with Knowledge OCR. Each service has its own source, package, image, and tests; no service imports, copies, installs, or mounts another service's application source.

The existing non-Knowledge service directories are placeholders. Implement Knowledge OCR as the first production job. Verify the general design with a second fake owner/type in tests; do not invent production jobs for services that are not implemented yet.

## Responsibility and data ownership

| Module | Owns | Must not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `job-service` | Dedicated job database, generic job lifecycle, atomic claims, progress, terminal state, RabbitMQ publish/replay, job status REST/WebSocket | PDFs, OCR drafts, document metadata, any originating service's database |
| `worker-service` | RabbitMQ consumption, handler selection, task execution, callbacks to job service and originating service | Job database, document database, MinIO credentials |
| Knowledge API | PDF upload/source, document metadata, OCR draft, document processing state, OCR result validation | `job` rows, job publishing, job status subscription |
| Future originating service | Its own business data, task input/output endpoints, job creation request | Direct access to the job database or worker internals |

Create a dedicated SurrealDB container/volume for `job-service` in local deployment (or an isolated database instance in other environments). Only `job-service` receives its credentials. Keep Knowledge's existing SurrealDB/MinIO for `document`, `ocr_draft`, and other Knowledge tables. Remove the `job` table, indexes, migrations, and schema application from Knowledge **only after** old jobs have been migrated or explicitly closed and all readers/writers have switched. Do not drop its volume or table during the first rollout.

## Job record and message contract

The central job record contains generic fields: globally unique `job_id`, `owner` (for example `knowledge`), `type` (for example `ocr_pdf`), opaque `subject_id` owned by the originating service (for Knowledge, the document ID), `status` (`queued`, `running`, `completed`, `failed`), `step`, `progress`, optional counts/error/result reference, `sequence`, timestamps, and `claim_id`. `claim_id` is an ownership guard, not a lease. Do not add Knowledge-specific `document_id` or `ocr_draft_id` fields to the central schema; use `subject_id` and an opaque `result_ref`. The Knowledge public compatibility adapter can map those fields to its current response shape.

New RabbitMQ messages use a versioned envelope such as `{"version":1,"owner":"knowledge","type":"ocr_pdf","job_id":"<central-job-id>"}`. Keep credentials, data, source URLs, and arbitrary callback URLs out of messages. Route on a shared direct exchange such as `jobs.v1`, using a durable queue per `(owner,type)` (for example `jobs.knowledge.ocr_pdf`). A worker instance subscribes only to queues for handlers it has enabled; separate queue/concurrency settings prevent long OCR work from blocking another type. Validate message version and fields; resolve owner URLs from worker configuration, never from a message. Unsupported/malformed messages go to a configured dead-letter path with an alert, not to an arbitrary handler.

The job service owns exchange/queue declarations and publishing. It creates a job durably, returns its ID, then schedules a best-effort publish without making the HTTP response wait for RabbitMQ confirmation. It scans `queued` jobs in bounded pages on the **first successful broker connection after startup** and after each broker reconnect, publishing their IDs; there is no periodic database scan. The first-connection scan covers a job-service process crash between saving a job and publishing it, including startup while RabbitMQ is down. Duplicate deliveries are safe because the job service atomically allows only one `queued -> running` claim. No outbox table, worker lease, OCR retry, or periodic sweep is introduced.

## End-to-end Knowledge OCR flow

1. Knowledge API validates and stores the PDF in MinIO, then saves its document record with `process_status=processing` in Knowledge's database. It generates a stable creation key tied to the document ID and calls `job-service` to create `(owner=knowledge,type=ocr_pdf,subject_id=document_id)`. The job service stores the queued job and returns the job ID. Knowledge returns its existing `202 {document_id, job_id}` only after both the document and central job exist. Knowledge never publishes to RabbitMQ directly.
2. The job service publishes the trigger. The worker consumes it, generates a unique claim ID, and asks the job service to claim. A duplicate/running/terminal job is acknowledged and ignored. After a successful durable claim, the worker ACKs the RabbitMQ message, preserving today's claim/ACK policy.
3. The Knowledge OCR handler fetches the PDF from an authenticated Knowledge internal endpoint and calls LM Studio page by page. It sends progress to the job service, whose database-backed status stream carries updates to the UI. Knowledge alone has MinIO credentials.
4. After OCR, the worker sends ordered page text to an idempotent Knowledge result endpoint. Knowledge validates pages, detects metadata, creates the stable OCR draft, and moves the document to review. Once Knowledge confirms its result was stored, the worker asks job service to mark the job completed with an opaque draft reference. A repeated result submission must return the same result without overwriting a draft a reviewer may have edited.
5. For a caught OCR error, the worker asks Knowledge to mark its document failed, then asks job service to mark the job failed with a bounded error. These callbacks are guarded by the claim ID and idempotent. No OCR retry is scheduled.

The document save and central job creation cannot share a database transaction. Make job creation idempotent on `(owner, creation_key)`, so an ambiguous HTTP response can be retried without creating a second job. If job creation definitely fails, Knowledge returns a non-`202` error and compensates or records the stored document/PDF for its existing orphan-cleanup process. If Knowledge dies between document storage and job creation, orphan cleanup must identify the document with no central job after a grace period. Do not report `202` before durable job creation.

Likewise, saving an OCR result in Knowledge and completing the central job cannot be one transaction. Apply the domain result **before** marking the job completed. Retry the same result and terminal calls after temporary network failures while the worker process is alive; idempotency makes an ambiguous response safe. If the worker dies between those two writes, the central job can remain `running` while a draft exists. This is an explicit consequence of the existing no-worker-recovery policy, not a completed job. A later recovery design would need a terminal reconciliation protocol.

## Interfaces

Define and document wire contracts; both sides keep their own local request/response models. No shared runtime package is required.

| Caller -> owner | Operation | Essential rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Originating service -> job service | Create job with owner, type, subject ID, creation key | Idempotently insert queued job and return ID; only registered owners/types are accepted |
| Worker -> job service | Claim, progress, complete, fail | Atomic state changes guarded by claim ID; terminal calls are idempotent |
| UI or compatibility adapter -> job service | Read snapshot and subscribe to status | Central job database is authoritative; status events carry increasing sequence |
| Worker -> originating service | Fetch task input, apply result, or mark domain failure | Owner validates claim/job context against job service or a signed short-lived claim proof; never trust an unverified `job_id`/`claim_id` header alone |

Use separate service credentials for each owner and the worker. The job service authenticates job creation and validates that the caller may create jobs only for its registered owner/type; it does not trust the caller-supplied owner string by itself. The worker's job-service credential may claim/update only enabled job types. For Knowledge input/result routes, use dedicated service authentication **and** validate that the job is currently claimed for that Knowledge document, using a job-service claim check or verifiable claim proof. This prevents a compromised or mistaken callback from reading arbitrary PDFs or changing unrelated documents. Bound source size, OCR page count/text size, and error length. Return stable `404`/`409`/`422` responses for absent, ownership-conflict, and invalid-input cases; treat temporary `5xx`/network errors separately.

Keep the existing Knowledge public `GET /v1/jobs/{job_id}` and `/v1/ws/jobs/{job_id}` URLs during migration by forwarding/translating central job snapshots/events. Remove Knowledge's SurrealDB job live query and job status hub once the new path is verified. Long term, the API gateway or UI may call job service directly, but that public routing change is outside this migration. Keep existing UI progress and `202` response fields.

## Implementation sequence

1. **Job service and store.** Create `services/job-service/` with its own SurrealDB schema, database container/volume, package, Dockerfile, config, tests, authenticated job creation/claim/progress/terminal/status interfaces, and database-backed WebSocket status updates. Implement owner/type registration, idempotent creation, claim guards, monotonic sequence/progress, and terminal idempotency. Test concurrent claims, duplicate create requests, wrong credentials/owner, ambiguous terminal responses, and ordered status events.
2. **Job publishing.** Move publisher/reconnect logic out of Knowledge into job service. Use versioned messages, distinct owner/type queues, a queued scan on the first successful broker connection, and a bounded reconnect scan. Test jobs saved while RabbitMQ is down, job-service restart after a saved-but-unpublished job, more queued rows than one batch, and duplicate publish. Keep the no-confirm policy requested for the current workflow.
3. **Knowledge producer and domain callbacks.** Change Knowledge upload to create a central job through a client after document/PDF persistence. Add idempotent, authenticated PDF input, OCR result, and domain-failure routes. Keep metadata detection and draft writes in Knowledge; verify claim ownership before each callback. Replace Knowledge job queries, metrics, and status subscription with central job-service reads/events. Preserve the public Knowledge job response/WebSocket contract. Add orphan-cleanup handling for a saved document with no central job.
4. **Shared worker.** Create `services/worker-service/` with a service-neutral RabbitMQ delivery loop, exact `(owner,type,version)` handler registry, owner adapters, and independent queue concurrency. Add Knowledge OCR as the first handler, with local PDF rendering/LM Studio code and the current 60-second elapsed-time deadline per page request. The worker has no SurrealDB or MinIO client/credentials. Test a fake second owner/type on its own queue without modifying the core loop.
5. **Cutover.** Deploy job service and its DB first, then Knowledge's new internal routes and compatibility status adapter, then the new worker. Stop the old Knowledge worker after in-flight jobs finish; do not run old and new workers against the same queue during migration. One-time migrate existing `queued`/`running`/terminal Knowledge jobs to the central store **with their IDs and sequence preserved**, or explicitly finish/close them before switching public reads. Reconcile the old RabbitMQ queue with those rows, drain/retire it, and run one-time central replay. Switch Knowledge uploads to central job creation only after status and worker paths work. Do not purge queues, old tables, or volumes until migration counts and UI-visible job IDs are verified.
6. **Cleanup and deployment.** Remove the old Knowledge `job` schema/table through an explicit later migration and delete its publisher, direct job database methods, worker code, and obsolete tests/settings. Keep Knowledge document and OCR draft data. Put job service, its DB, RabbitMQ, and worker in the repository-level `deploy/docker-compose.yml` (currently a placeholder); connect Knowledge through the shared network. Update environment examples and workflow docs. Verify images build independently and only job service has job-DB credentials.

## Acceptance checks and limits

- A Knowledge PDF upload returns `202` with a central job ID. No `job` row is created in Knowledge's database; one queued job exists in the dedicated job database. The new worker produces a reviewable Knowledge OCR draft and the UI receives progress through the existing URLs.
- A duplicate create request returns the same job ID. Duplicate RabbitMQ triggers produce one claim/processing run. A job-service restart or RabbitMQ reconnect publishes queued jobs without a periodic scan.
- Knowledge callback authentication/claim validation blocks another job from reading its PDF or writing its draft. An OCR request exceeding 60 seconds and another caught error produce failed document/job states without retry.
- A fake second owner/type executes through a separate queue and adapter while Knowledge OCR is busy. No Knowledge-specific identifiers or fields appear in the generic job schema or delivery core.
- Existing job IDs remain readable during cutover; migration or explicit closure is verified before deleting Knowledge's job table. The old and new worker do not compete for the same queue.

Accepted limits: a silent RabbitMQ publish loss without a reported disconnect may leave a job queued until job-service restart or broker reconnect because publisher confirms are disabled. A worker/container death after claim and ACK, or a callback outage that outlives that worker, can leave a job running. The job and document databases cannot commit atomically; the idempotency and ordering above limit inconsistent states but do not provide automatic worker-death recovery. Show the stored state accurately in the UI.
